**Definition of done (ROADMAP):** stop *asserting* that weight-based memory is private and start
**measuring** it — what weights actually buy over prompting, whether separately-taught personas
stay isolated under adversarial collision, and whether an adversary can extract taught facts
through a toggle that only ever controlled availability.

four phases are verified, the pre-registration discipline held end-to-end, and the milestone closed
on a `FAILURE` verdict published unsoftened — which is the discipline working, not failing. One
cross-phase gap is open: a scope limit Phase 19 authored against Phase 18 never reached Phase 18's
published record.

## What this milestone actually established

The pre-registration discipline held for four consecutive phases and 63 published artifacts, and it
was the mechanism that authored Phase 19 rather than a judgement made after seeing the numbers:
`erasure_is_worth_attempting(92, 104, 0, 104)` returned `True` against a rule committed at `23a830c`
**before Phase 16 ran**, and the same gate returns MOOT on a null attack, an equal base arm and a
narrow margin. Every headline is re-derivable from committed raw records through committed
functions — Phase 16's report and Phase 18's entire 48,511-byte body both re-render **byte-identically**.

And the milestone's own strongest finding is a negative one it published against itself: **selective
erasure is not selective at 331,776 parameters.** (a) cleared exactly on its boundary with zero
headroom while all seven gated non-targets failed, four at total generation loss, and 77.6% of the
dialogue adaptation was destroyed — while the rank/exposure instrument reported all seven ruined
facts at rank 1 and ceiling exposure at every checkpoint. That co-headline is the source of B1: it
carries a retroactive scope limit onto Phase 18's own rank-based readings, and closing B1 is what
makes that limit reachable by the reader Phase 18's own artifact invites.
